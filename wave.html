3D prints · wall panel

Wave panel

A parametric slat relief for behind the desk. One continuous wave, 48 by 14 inches, printed as twelve tiles and painted as one object.

On the wall

Twelve tiles print flat, dowel together on their shared edges, glue to a board and take one coat of matte paint as a single object. The wave runs continuously across every seam, and the seams themselves sit in its troughs.

run48 × 14 in · 6 × 2 tiles of 203 × 178 mm
slats9 per tile · 22.6 mm pitch · 6.4 → 4.8 mm, printed hollow
crestsknife edge · 45° flanks following the wave
wavetwo ridges + swell, short wave, diagonal wave, vertical ripple · peaks on the flanks · calm behind the monitor · ends taper into the wall
print0.8 mm nozzle · 0.32 mm · 1 wall · 12% · ≈ 2 kg PLA+

Two builds before it

The same wave, printed three ways. These two are kept because the reasons they lost are the design argument: filament cost first, then fin thickness.

Lighter

Chunky hollow fins in place of thin solid ones, which answered both the brittleness and the cost. Still 2.8 kg, and the slats read as a fence more than a wave.

run50 × 20 in · 5 × 2 tiles of 250 mm
slats14 per tile · 17.9 mm pitch · 6 → 4.5 mm hollow
print0.4 mm nozzle · 2 walls · 15% · ≈ 2.8 kg
STL

First cut

Twenty solid slats a tile, closest to the reference panel and the reason the project nearly stopped: roughly 6 kg of filament, and fins thin enough to snap on the way to the wall.
