Digital · lectr.bid ↗

Auction valuations
traced to realized sales.

lectr values upcoming auction lots from past sales and rebuilds itself every night.

  • Crawls eighteen auction houses nightly, one isolated job each.
  • Reconciles hammer and buyer’s premium so prices compare across houses.
  • Values upcoming lots from realized comparables, with a band and a confidence.
  • Records every call before the hammer and grades it after.

the problem · corpus

Eighteen houses, one book.

Each house has its own crawler and book segment. A failed house can’t touch another house’s data, and a crawl that shrinks its own segment can’t overwrite the last good one.

Christie’s · Sotheby’s · Bonhams · Phillips · Wright · Rago · LAMA · Goldin · REA · Huggins & Scott · Lelands · Memory Lane · Hake’s · RR Auction · SCP · MLB Auctions · NFL Auction · Love of the Game

the build · nightly

Crawl, assemble, deploy.

Crawl runs one GitHub Actions job per house in parallel. Assemble merges segments, runs the value engine and market indices, and writes the payload deploy builds from. Stages pass same-run artifacts; write-once versions in Cloudflare R2 are the durable copy.

the build · gates

Nothing publishes unless three gates agree.

Corpus shrink. If the merged book is more than 10% smaller than the last published one, the last good version stays live.

Price bleed. A broken feed can stamp one price across a batch. The sentinel blocks only repeat-price clusters within a sale date that were not there yesterday, since a 25-year book carries hundreds of honest bid-increment ties. Any single price on 500+ lots is refused regardless.

[assemble] sentinel: 87 repeat signatures, 82 poison (0 NEW vs baseline of 87)
[validate] Directional signal: VALIDATED — ships (monotonic beat-rate gradient)

Engine holdout. Each release is replayed against a temporal holdout. Lots called cheap must beat their estimate more often than lots called rich, by a minimum spread (current spread in the record below). A local wobble marks the signal degraded; a failed spread blocks the release.

How we built the price-movement engine ↗

the run · speed

From 130 minutes to 52, without changing a number.

Two steps dominated assemble: repeat-sale grouping and per-maker indices. Both were rewritten, with outputs verified identical against the full book.

  • Repeat-sale grouping, indexing only lots that can match1,660s → 3s
  • Per-maker hedonic indices, worker pool + covariance only for columns read1,700s → 87s
  • Assemble stage71–95 min → 33 min
  • Full nightly, weekday110–130 min → 52 min
