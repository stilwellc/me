Digital · lectr.bid ↗

Auction intelligence,
with every price traceable to a sale.

lectr keeps over 1.1 million auction lots from eighteen houses and puts a value on what is coming up next. It runs itself every night. This is how it is built, and what it took to make it fast.

  • Crawls eighteen auction houses every night, one isolated job per house.
  • Reconciles hammer and buyer’s premium so prices compare across houses.
  • Values upcoming lots from realized comparables, with a band and a confidence.
  • Writes every call down before the hammer and grades it after.

the problem · corpus

Eighteen houses, one book.

Each house has its own crawler and its own segment of the book. A house that fails can’t touch another house’s data, and a crawl that collapses its own segment refuses to overwrite the last good one.

Prices carry their basis. Hammer and buyer’s premium are reconciled per house so a Sotheby’s result and a Goldin result mean the same thing in a comparison.

the build · nightly

Crawl, assemble, ship. Staged, isolated, retryable.

One GitHub Actions workflow, three stages. The crawl is a matrix, one job per house, all in parallel. Assemble is the only job that sees the whole book: it merges every segment, runs the value engine and the market indices, and writes the served payload. Deploy pulls that payload and builds the site. Data moves between stages as same-run artifacts, with write-once versions in Cloudflare R2 behind a tiny pointer as the durable copy.

the build · gates

Nothing publishes unless three gates agree.

Every input is a scrape of someone else’s website, so the gates are integrity controls: the pipeline treats its own data as untrusted until it proves otherwise.

Corpus shrink. If the merged book is more than 10% smaller than the last published one, an outage somewhere returned empty, and the last good version stays live.

Price bleed. A broken feed stamps one price across a batch. The sentinel looks for a price repeating on many lots inside one sale date, and it is a delta gate: it only stops the publish for clusters that were not there yesterday, because a 25-year book legitimately carries hundreds of honest bid-increment ties. A backstop still refuses any single price on 500+ lots, baseline or not.

[assemble] sentinel: 87 repeat signatures, 82 poison (0 NEW vs baseline of 87)
[validate] Directional signal: VALIDATED — ships (monotonic beat-rate gradient)

Engine holdout. Before the value engine ships, it is replayed against a temporal holdout. The directional signal must carry: lots the engine calls cheap must beat their estimate more often than lots it calls rich, with a spread wide enough to mean something; the current spread is in the record below. A local wobble degrades the signal and says so; a failed spread blocks the release.

the run · speed

From 130 minutes to 52, without changing a number.

Profiling a healthy night showed the assemble stage was almost entirely two things: a repeat-sale grouper and the per-maker price indices. Both were rewritten so the outputs are provably identical, checked offline against the full book before they shipped.

  • Repeat-sale grouping, only lots that can physically match are indexed1,660s → 3s
  • Per-maker hedonic indices, worker pool + covariance solved for the columns actually read1,700s → 87s
  • Assemble stage71–95 min → 33 min
  • Full nightly, weekday110–130 min → 52 min
