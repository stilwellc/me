What's actually happening
tonight.

Every city has more going on than anyone can find. The listings are scattered across venue calendars, community boards, and a few good newsletters, and half of them don't carry a real date. Soirée gathers them, fixes the dates, and shows you tonight.

the problem

Dates are the hard part.

Event sources are inconsistent in one specific way: many post recurring or open-ended listings with no date at all. Early on, fewer than half the scraped events carried a real date. Sorting a feed by "date" when half the dates are placeholders produces garbage.

Soirée's rule is simple and strict. Events with real dates sort first, by date. Placeholder dates render as ongoing rather than pretending to be a day. A placeholder event that stops appearing at its source is automatically marked past, so nothing needs manual cleanup and history is preserved.

the sources

Scraped from the people who post them.

Five scrapers, each tuned to its source: NYC For Free, The Local Girl for Hoboken and Jersey City, Time Out New York, and the museum calendars at MoMA, the Guggenheim, and the New Museum. Each source gets its own success-rate target and its own timeout, because a slow community board and a fast museum feed shouldn't share one.
