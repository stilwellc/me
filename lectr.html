lectr watches eighteen auction houses, keeps every lot they have ever sold, and puts a value on what is coming up next. It runs itself every night. This is how it is built, and what it took to make it fast.

the corpus

Eighteen houses, one book.

Each house has its own crawler and its own segment of the corpus. A house that fails can’t touch another house’s data, and a crawl that collapses its own segment refuses to overwrite the last good one.

Prices carry their basis. Hammer and buyer’s premium are reconciled per house so a Sotheby’s result and a Goldin result mean the same thing in a comparison.

the nightly

Crawl, assemble, ship. Staged, isolated, retryable.

One GitHub Actions workflow, three stages. The crawl is a matrix, one job per house, all in parallel. Assemble is the only job that sees the whole corpus: it reunions every segment, runs the value engine and the market indices, and writes the served payload. Deploy pulls that payload and builds the site. Data moves between stages as same-run artifacts, with write-once versions in Cloudflare R2 behind a tiny pointer as the durable copy.

the gates

Nothing publishes unless three gates agree.

Corpus shrink. If the reunioned book is more than 10% smaller than the last published one, an outage somewhere returned empty, and last-good stays live.

Price bleed. A broken feed stamps one price across a batch. The sentinel looks for a price repeating on many lots inside one sale date, and it is a delta gate: it only stops the publish for clusters that were not there yesterday, because a 25-year book legitimately carries hundreds of honest bid-increment ties. A backstop still refuses any single price on 500+ lots, baseline or not.

[assemble] sentinel: 87 repeat signatures, 82 poison (0 NEW vs baseline of 87)
[validate] Directional signal: VALIDATED — ships (monotonic beat-rate gradient)

Engine holdout. Before the value engine ships, it is replayed against a temporal holdout. The directional signal must carry: lots the engine calls cheap must beat their estimate more often than lots it calls rich, with a spread wide enough to mean something; the current spread is in the record below. A local wobble degrades the signal and says so; a failed spread blocks the release.

the speed work

From 130 minutes to 52, without changing a number.

Profiling a healthy night showed the assemble stage was almost entirely two things: a repeat-sale grouper and the per-maker price indices. Both were rewritten so the outputs are provably identical, checked offline against the full corpus before they shipped.

  • Repeat-sale grouping, only lots that can physically match are indexed1,660s → 3s
  • Per-maker hedonic indices, worker pool + covariance solved for the columns actually read1,700s → 87s
  • Assemble stage71–95 min → 33 min
  • Full nightly, weekday110–130 min → 52 min
